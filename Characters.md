1. Noah "The Enigma" [M]
Role: Main Character
Traits: Observant, manipulative, and unpredictable.
Description: Noah might come off as unassuming, but his background tells another story. Formerly a psychology student with an affinity for understanding criminal minds, he now serves The Captain. Tasked with sabotage, Noah's psychological expertise allows him to read and manipulate the group, making every Deck Trial a layered mind game. His actions are often unpredictable, reminiscent of Kokichi or Nagito from Danganronpa. Underneath his friendly demeanor lies a mastermind, playing both sides for his ultimate goal.
Backstory: Previously a psychology student, Noah had a budding interest in criminal minds. His thesis, which delved deep into the psyche of manipulators and sociopaths, caught the attention of the Undernet elites. This knowledge, paired with his strategic thinking, made him the perfect mole for The Captain.

The character is depicted with fiery red layered hair that suggests both wildness and grace, brushing just past his ears. His face has a defined jawline, elevated cheekbones, straight narrow nose, and slender arching eyebrows. The eyes, striking in nature, are adorned with slim, matte black rectangular glasses. He wears a black choker with a central silver pendant, complemented by assorted silver chain necklaces, a white shirt tucked and secured by a dark belt creating a draped effect, a fitted black jacket with textured fabric and white fur, glossy black pants with distinct silver chains, and dark leather boots with silver buckles if visible. The overall aura is a mix of rebellion, elegance, confidence, and intrigue.

Noah "The Enigma" Motive:

Before the cataclysm, Noah was an ambitious psychology student. His research focused on the darkest recesses of the human mind, specifically the motivations behind those who committed heinous acts. His interests were academic at first; he wanted to understand to prevent, to rehabilitate, and to change.

However, as the world began to crumble around him, the structure and resources that kept many in check were gone. Crime, especially on the DarkWeb, became rampant. The worst parts of humanity were on full display, and it was here that Noah began to see patterns and links between the acts of criminals and the society that had now turned to chaos.

Noah became disillusioned. He felt that, rather than being anomalies, these criminals were merely a product of society's true nature, unmasked by the cataclysm. Instead of rehabilitation, Noah began to believe that what society viewed as "dark tendencies" were innate. That these tendencies weren't deviations but were instead the true nature of humanity, merely suppressed by societal norms and structures.

When The Captain approached Noah, it was with a shared vision. The Captain offered Noah a platform to validate his theory. If Noah could manipulate a group of survivors into turning on one another, under the guise of a game, it would prove that, given the right circumstances, anyone could become a monster.

For Noah, this was a grand experiment. By serving as The Captain's mole, he could push and prod the other participants, using his knowledge of psychology to create mistrust, paranoia, and, ultimately, betrayal. Each Deck Trial became a proving ground for his theories. If the group failed to cooperate, if they succumbed to suspicion and fear, it would further solidify Noah's belief in the innate darkness of humanity.

In essence, Noah's motive isn't purely for personal gain or loyalty to The Captain. Instead, it's driven by a need to validate a deeply-held belief about human nature. He sees himself as a conductor of an orchestra, with each participant playing their part in a symphony of chaos and betrayal.

Noah's actions as "The Enigma" aren't purely for the spectacle of the game but rather to test his theory. His unpredictable nature keeps the group on their toes, but his underlying motive is always consistent – to expose the darkness within.
